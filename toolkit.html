Decision trees

Three questions, asked in order, that decide how a site enters the database. Work down them on site — most places answer within a minute.

1

Case study, opportunity, or both?

Has something already changed here that another site could learn from?

Yes, and it works → Case study Yes, but it is mid-change → Case study, status: emerging No, but it could → Opportunity

A site can be both. St Patrick's Wapping records two working interventions and a live opportunity in its next phases.

2

What type of opportunity is it?

Score the site on the four dimensions. Whichever are weak name the opportunity.

Use — the space sits empty or single-purpose Publicness — you cannot get in, or cannot tell that you may Interaction — you can get in, but nothing happens between people Temporality — it is open, but only for an hour a week

High publicness with low interaction — open but passive — is the most common and cheapest category to improve.

3

Semi-secular or not?

Does worship, remembrance or consecration still shape what the space can hold?

Active worship → time-share rather than partition; protect a quiet room Semi-secular → sacred traces remain; match the register, keep moves reversible Secular → deconsecrated; the constraint is heritage fabric, not liturgy

This answer decides the compatibility list below — not every good use fits every sacred site.

Assessment 1 — the publicness ladder

Rate the site as it behaves on an ordinary Tuesday, not as its policy or website claims. A site earns a level when it meets all of that level's checks.

A site can hold two ratings at once — Paddington Old Cemetery's grounds are L5 while its chapels are L1. Record the gradient: it is often exactly where the opportunity is.

Assessment 2 — the interaction test

Publicness gets people in; interaction is what happens between them. Watch the site for half an hour and score each question Low, Medium or High.

QuestionWhat to look for
Activity mixAre different things happening at once — eating, playing, praying, working, resting? Mixed activity creates reasons for different people to share space.
Design for engagementDo seating, tables and thresholds put people within speaking distance, or keep them in parallel?
LingeringCan someone stay without paying or explaining themselves? Count seats, shelter, toilets, warmth.
Encounter across differenceDo ages, cultures and incomes actually overlap here, or do groups use the site in shifts?
FacilitationIs anyone — staff, volunteers, a postmaster, a gardener — playing host? Facilitation converts co-presence into contact.
InvitationDo the edges say come in? Open doors, visible activity, legible entrances, programme posters.

Read the two scores together on the publicness × interaction chart shown on every case study page.

Assessment 3 — the compatibility list

Not every good use fits every sacred site. Test the match on four fronts, then look up a case study that has already tried it.

TestAskPrecedent in this database
SacrednessDoes the use respect worship rhythms, consecrated ground and remembrance? Can sacred and secular be timetabled rather than partitioned?The Sherriff Centre closes for Sunday worship; its Lady Chapel stays quiet all week.
RegisterDoes the use match the emotional temperature of the place? Cemeteries carry quiet cultural uses better than loud commercial ones.Tower Hamlets Cemetery Park layers learning and nature over remembrance.
FabricIs the intervention reversible in listed fabric? Could furniture or programming achieve it before building does?Reordering at St Stephen Walbrook; reversible galleries at the Garden Museum.
EconomyDoes the use pay its way or need permanent subsidy? Can trading fund the community offer?Grand Junction's venue income funds its free programme.

Assessment 4 — the design checklist

Publicness and interaction say how a place behaves. This says how it is made. Ten themes, each a short set of questions, taken from The Glass-House's Making Buildings Work for Your Community and Empowering Design Practices' Explore Design — the standard prompts to carry into any conversation with a congregation, a partner or an architect.
