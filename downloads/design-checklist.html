The design checklist

Ten themes to take to site — two sides of paper

After The Glass-House Community Led Design, Making Buildings Work for Your Community: Design, Refurbishment and Retrofit (2011), and Empowering Design Practices, Explore Design: Community Buildings. The questions are paraphrased; the ten themes are theirs. Each theme carries a note on what it means in a building that is also sacred.

How to use it. Do not answer this from memory. Walk the site with two or three other people, score each theme separately, then compare — the disagreements are the most useful output. Take it at a time when the building is doing something ordinary, not at its best.

After the walk

Three questions to answer together, before anyone proposes anything:

  1. Which two themes scored lowest? That is where the design work is — not where the enthusiasm is.
  2. What is the cheapest thing that would move one of them? Small changes achieve a lot, and testing before committing is the most useful thing a group can do.
  3. What would you have to know before spending money? Write it down. That list is the start of your options appraisal.

Then take it into the site studio, which will rank the library's sixty interventions against your scores and name the permission route and funding for each.
